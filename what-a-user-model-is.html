Chapter 1

What a portable user model is

Why an agent that only remembers what it is told knows little about you, what a user model adds, what the products that exist do and do not offer, and how to decide which parts to buy and which to build.

After this chapter you should be able to
  • Say what a user model holds that an assistant's own memory does not.
  • Name the four jobs such a system has, and what each must guarantee.
  • Read a product's licence and stated costs before building on it, and tell a maker's claim from a measurement.
  • Decide, part by part, what to buy and what to build.

1.1The problem: an assistant that knows only what it is told

A personal assistant with a memory remembers what its owner says to it. That is a small part of a working day. It does not know which document was open ten minutes ago, which project took the morning, or how its owner phrases a refusal, unless somebody types that in. So every request starts with the owner explaining their own situation.

The alternative is to learn it where it happens. Nearly everything a knowledge worker does passes through one computer, and the computer can say which window had the keyboard, what text was in it, and what was typed.

1.2The concepts

Definition
User model
A kept description of one person, built from what they do: what they were just working on, their projects and the people they deal with, how they write, and over time their goals, priorities, preferences and commitments.
Definition
Portable
Held in files the person owns and can read, edit, back up and move, and offered to any agent through an open protocol, so that it belongs to no single assistant.
Definition
Provenance
A mark on a piece of information saying how it is known: who wrote it, how it was observed, and when.

Such a system has four jobs. The rest of the book takes them in turn.

Table 1.1. The four jobs and what each must guarantee.
JobWhat it doesWhat it must guaranteeChapter

DistilTurns raw records into a kept model.Facts about the owner come from the owner.Chapter 4, Chapter 5

1.3What exists

The rule followed here is to buy a part unless building it is verifiably better. So the first step was to read what could be used. All of this was read on 23 November 2026.

What went wrong

The first search for screenpipe's licence turned up three different answers on three directory sites, one of them "MIT". The licence file in its own repository settled it: older versions were MIT, the current code is not. A directory page describes a product as it once was. Read the file.

Summary
  • A user model is learnt from what a person does, not from what they tell an assistant.
  • It has four jobs: capture, distil, store, serve. Each has one guarantee that matters more than its features.
  • What exists was read first. The parts with a good off-the-shelf answer were bought; the three with none were built.
Exercises
  1. Pick one product in the second table and find the page that states its licence. What use would you need a paid plan for?
  2. The table of jobs gives one guarantee per job. Propose a fifth job and its guarantee. What would break if it were missing?
  3. A maker states that its recorder uses "5 to 20%" of a processor. Design the measurement you would make before believing or dismissing that figure for your own machine.
